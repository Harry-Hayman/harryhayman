Confetti is hanging in the air above the scissors in my photograph from Plate & Social. The ribbon carries the letters DEBCC, balloons frame the doorway, and overhead the sign reads Good Food Better Company. What a damn great day. I'm Harry Hayman, and I went to Wilmington to celebrate my dear friend Jon Christopher Webb and his family opening a restaurant. Seeing them reach that moment made me happy in a way that anyone who has watched a friend build something will understand. Then I look at my other photograph, the one of the dining room before the ceremony. Plates on the tables. Napkins folded. Chairs waiting. That's the picture I keep coming back to, because I want people sitting in those chairs long after the confetti has been swept up.

## Friday on Greenhill Avenue

Plate & Social is at 504 Greenhill Avenue in Wilmington, Delaware, the address on the [restaurant's own website](https://plateandsocial.com/). The Delaware Black Chamber of Commerce's [ribbon cutting announcement on Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/e/plate-and-socials-ribbon-cutting-ceremony-tickets-2002833074641) scheduled the celebration for Friday, October 30, 2026, from 5 to 6 p.m. That's the occasion I attended and photographed.

There had already been an opening. [Out & About's November restaurant coverage](https://outandaboutnow.com/bites-november-2026/) reports that Plate & Social celebrated its grand opening on October 17. I like keeping those occasions straight. Getting a restaurant open and gathering the community to celebrate it are both worth marking, and this Friday brought the chamber to the doorway.

The front photograph catches the instant everybody has been waiting for. Hands gather around the scissors, colored paper fills the frame, and someone in the foreground holds up a phone. A red carpet runs along the entrance. The sign is big enough that there's no question whose day this is. Plate & Social. The name is right over everybody's heads.

## Jon and the Family Behind the Restaurant

Jon is my friend, so of course I was excited for him. But the family effort is what I wanted to celebrate. The restaurant's [account of its founders](https://plateandsocial.com/about) names Jon alongside Andrea Webb, Lamar Waples and Christina Waples. That is the team the business identifies publicly, and they deserve the credit together.

Having spent most of my adult life in restaurants, I can't look at an opening without thinking about everything it took to get there. The permits, the staffing, the construction, the menu, and all the little decisions that nobody eating dinner should have to think about. I know that work from this business. Jon and his family have put a tremendous amount into making this place happen, and I have enormous respect for that.

I don't know every decision they made along the way. What I could see was the result, and what I felt was how glad the people there seemed to be to welcome us. There is a difference between congratulating somebody on an idea and standing outside the door of the thing they have actually built. I was looking at the door.

## The Room Before the Crowd

My interior photograph gives you time to look around. Small gold colored lamps stand on pale tabletops, with white plates, clear glasses and dark blue napkins set beside them. The chairs have light upholstery and curved wooden frames. Behind them, a long bar runs across the picture, lined with dark wooden stools.

Two screens above the bar show food photographs. Glass pendant lights hang from the dark ceiling, and between the screens a circular frame sits within a rack holding glassware. A small illuminated Plate & Social sign is above it all. There's greenery too, hanging at the left and trailing around the rack.

I notice the table settings because I've spent so much time getting rooms ready for people. Each place setting means somebody has thought about the person who will sit there. My photograph catches those places ready and waiting, which is a perfectly ordinary restaurant sight until the restaurant belongs to your friend. Then you start imagining who will come through the door.

I wrote that the atmosphere was really nice and the hospitality wonderful. Those were my impressions of being there. Looking back at the room, I can point to the lamps and the chairs, but the welcome is the part that makes me want to go back.

## Good Food, Better Company

The promise on the sign is also the idea the owners describe on their website. In the [owners' account of Plate & Social](https://plateandsocial.com/about), the restaurant brings people together for food, family gatherings, time with friends and connections with colleagues. They describe a menu of comfort food, brunch, entr?es, cocktails and appetizers to share. The [published food menu](https://plateandsocial.com/food-menu) gives a prospective guest the details before choosing a visit.

I appreciate that the social part has a place in the plan. The [owners also describe plans](https://plateandsocial.com/about) for live music, DJs, local bands, comedy and networking events. Those are their stated plans, and I'm interested in seeing what they do with the room. A restaurant gives you a reason to invite someone somewhere. Add a performance or an occasion, and you have another reason to make that call.

Still, I don't need a special event to recommend the place. Great food and people who seem happy to have you there are enough to get my attention. That was what I wrote after the visit. I wasn't trying to describe every item on a menu. I was excited that my friend and his family had opened somewhere I wanted other people to experience.

## The Chamber at the Door

The [Delaware Black Chamber of Commerce](https://debcc.org/) describes its work as helping Black businesses compete in Delaware and connecting entrepreneurs with assistance, relationships and opportunities. At this celebration, that work had a very visible form. Its initials were on the ribbon being cut outside a new family restaurant.

I gave the chamber a shoutout in my post because I like seeing an organization get behind entrepreneurs this way. Opening a business is a big undertaking. Having people arrive to recognize it, introduce it and help others find it is useful. I want a chamber to be close enough to a business that its name turns up at the front door.

There's more behind the ribbon. The chamber's [programs and partnerships page](https://debcc.org/programs/) includes business education, mentoring and networking alongside celebrations and ribbon cuttings. It describes EnrichDelaware as a program developed with Interise that brings business owners together for learning and peer support. I'm glad to see the practical work and the celebrations on the same page.

My interest here is straightforward. I want Jon and his family to have people they can call, and I want people hearing about Plate & Social for the first time to know where to go. Watching a chamber help celebrate an opening makes me think about what we can each do next. For most of us, that next thing is within reach: arrange a meal.

## A Table for the Next Visit

The part of my post I most want people to act on is the invitation to go back. Bring friends. Enjoy yourselves. If you like it, tell somebody who is deciding where to eat. I can write congratulations all day, but I'd much rather help put a restaurant on somebody's calendar.

And check that calendar against the restaurant's hours. The [restaurant's current hours](https://plateandsocial.com/) are Thursday from 4 to 9 p.m. and Friday through Sunday from 10 a.m. to 9 p.m., with Monday through Wednesday closed. For a visit now, plan around those days and check the site before heading over. I want the enthusiasm from the opening to turn into another dinner, another gathering of friends, another reason for somebody to walk through that doorway.

The restaurant has an [online reservation page](https://plateandsocial.com/reservations) where you can choose a date, time and party size. That makes the invitation easy to turn into an actual visit. There are the lamps, there are the plates, and there are the chairs in my photograph. Pick a day and bring someone you'd like to sit across from.

Jon, congratulations to you and your entire family. I can't wait to see what you do with this place. Now let's get some butts in seats.
