On Wednesday night, October 28, an aerialist hung in a hoop high above the floor at FringeArts, lit purple and green, with a row of arched windows glowing behind and a crowd below. I'm Harry Hayman, and that is the photo my post about FEASTIVAL 2026 opens with, because it says what the night was: the weird stuff, hanging over the food. FEASTIVAL is the annual benefit for FringeArts. Restaurants and bars set up tables around the building, artists perform among them, and the proceeds go to the organization that makes room for those artists in the first place. This is the longer version of my post: where FringeArts lives, where FEASTIVAL came from, whose tables are in my photos, and why a night like this belongs in any serious conversation about Philadelphia's creative economy.

## A Wednesday Night at FringeArts

FEASTIVAL 2026 ran on Wednesday, October 28, at [FringeArts and Fringe Bar](https://fringearts.com/event/feastival-2026/), 140 North Columbus Boulevard at Race Street. FringeArts listed VIP entry at 6 p.m. and the main event at 7, with tickets at $200 for general admission and $350 for VIP, creative cocktail attire, and Cristina Martinez, Ari Miller and Rue Landau as cohosts. [PhillyVoice's preview](https://www.phillyvoice.com/feastival-2026-fringearts-philadelphia-restaurants-bars-food-drinks-live-performances/) put the main event at 7 to 9:30 p.m., counted more than 25 restaurants and bars, and said FEASTIVAL would turn FringeArts into "a mix of tasting room and performance space."

The photos in my post carry both halves of that line. The second shows the Fringe Bar tent glowing at night. The third is a FEASTIVAL sign marking the way to check in, on a garden path washed in green light, and the fourth is the hall itself, bar and crowd under the same green. A guitarist plays under strings of lights in one frame. Another frame catches an aerialist wound around a spiral hung above the stage. The post ends on the lit garden outside.

## What FringeArts Is, and Where It Lives

[FringeArts](https://fringearts.com/about/) calls itself Philadelphia's home for contemporary performance. Its programming runs through the year in a renovated historic building on the Delaware River waterfront, and every September it presents the annual Fringe Festival. That festival began in 1997 with 60 groups performing over five days, according to [WHYY](https://whyy.org/articles/nick-stuccio-philadelphia-fringe-festival-step-down). In 2013 the Philadelphia Live Arts Festival and Philly Fringe took the single name FringeArts, an announcement made at the groundbreaking for the building and [reported by Philadelphia Magazine](https://www.phillymag.com/2013/02/25/philadelphia-live-arts-philly-fringe-venue-fringe-arts/).

The building has a past worth knowing. Philadelphia Magazine described it as a 1903 hydrant pumping station at Columbus Avenue and Race Street, and [Visit Philly](https://www.visitphilly.com/things-to-do/attractions/fringearts/) places it beneath the Benjamin Franklin Bridge, with exposed brick walls, high ceilings and large windows that keep the original character. In my photo of the Fork table, brick washed in green light and a tall arched window show behind the plates. Old infrastructure with a second job, and the job is culture: that is an argument I keep making, and this building makes it without a word.

## Where FEASTIVAL Came From

FEASTIVAL is the benefit that keeps that work going. Restaurants, bars and specialty food purveyors set up tables, artists perform around them, and the money goes to FringeArts. PhillyVoice calls it FringeArts' largest annual fundraiser, with proceeds supporting FringeArts programming and the Fringe Festival.

[FringeArts says](https://fringearts.com/event/feastival-2026/) FEASTIVAL has supported it "since its inception in 2010," and a [PA Eats announcement from July 2010](https://www.paeats.org/news/2010/first-annual-feastival-benefit-brings-together-the-citys-top-chefs/) announced the first annual FEASTIVAL for Wednesday, September 15, 2010, at the Festival Hub in Northern Liberties, with dozens of restaurants and bars listed, as a benefit for the Philadelphia Live Arts Festival and Philly Fringe. It named three hosts: Audrey Claire Taichman, Michael Solomonov and Stephen Starr.

Later coverage tells the founding story a few ways, so I will give each as it ran. [Philadelphia Magazine](https://www.phillymag.com/the-scene/2013/09/13/feastival-2013/) wrote in 2013 that FEASTIVAL was founded by those three culinary luminaries, and that year more than 75 of the city's top restaurants and bars gathered at Pier 9 "under the shimmering lights of the Ben Franklin Bridge." The [Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/blogs/out_and_about/FEASTival-Funk-food-and-fancy-footwork-Phila-2015-sixth-annual.html) called Taichman "the force behind the event" in 2015 and said she began it in 2009 as a way to diversify funding for FringeArts, a year earlier than the 2010 date FringeArts and PA Eats give. That sixth edition counted 76 participating bars and restaurants and promised to raise nearly $500,000 for FringeArts. By 2019, when FEASTIVAL moved to Cherry Street Pier, [PhillyVoice described it](https://www.phillyvoice.com/feastival-2019-tickets-cherry-street-pier/) as founded a decade earlier by Taichman and reported that the party had raised more than $3 million for FringeArts.

## The Tables in My Photos

Eight tables are named in the photos in my post, and I am going to stick to what the cards say.

At [High Street](https://highstreetonmarket.com/), the card reads Green Tomato Caesar, with gluten and dairy noted and a line that it can be made vegetarian. A [Fork](https://www.forkrestaurant.com/) sign towers beside rows of small plates, with the hall glowing below a railing. At [Amy's Pastelillos](https://6abc.com/post/amys-pastelillos-philadelphia-fishtown-amaryllis-rivera-nassar/10955388/), a Puerto Rican flag sits among white pumpkins and candles beside cups of ube tembleque with salsa macha, marked vegan and containing nuts.

The drinks had cards too. [Twisted Tail](https://thetwistedtail.com/)'s card says Select Your Poison and lists Maker's Mark whiskey, ginger, amaro, lemon and honey. [Post Haste](https://6abc.com/post/post-haste-philadelphia-cocktail-bar-restaurants-kensington/13901674/)'s card lists a cocktail named Amateur Corn, with [Bluebird Distilling](https://www.bluebirddistilling.com/) printed at the foot. A chalkboard from [Grace & Proper](https://www.graceandproper.com/) announces a spiced pumpkin tequila cocktail. For dessert, [Wilder](https://www.wilderphilly.com/)'s card reads Triple Chocolate Tiramisu, and [Franklin Fountain](https://franklinfountain.com/)'s reads Faux Mint Chip, beside a carton stamped Franklin Ice Cream.

Read the neighborhoods off those names and you get a map of the city: Old City for Fork and Franklin Fountain, Washington Square West for High Street, Rittenhouse Square for Wilder, the Italian Market for Grace & Proper, South Second Street for Twisted Tail, East Kensington for Post Haste and Fishtown for Amy's Pastelillos, with a Phoenixville distillery's name on one of the cards. One building on the waterfront pulled all of it together on a Wednesday night. How do you get that many kitchens and bars to show up for one cause? Anyone who has worked in hospitality knows that is not a small ask, and FEASTIVAL has been making it since 2010.

PhillyVoice's September preview also listed South Philly Barbacoa/Casa México, a.kitchen, Irwin's, Kiddo, Manong and Habibi among the participants. I have no cards for those in my photos, so I will not describe their tables.

## The Weird Stuff Is the Point

In my post I wrote that I love the weird stuff: the unconventional, the experimental, the people with an idea that makes everybody else tilt their heads. FEASTIVAL puts that taste on a stage and hangs it from the ceiling. FringeArts describes the night as "a night of spectacular acts, bites + drinks from some of Philadelphia's most inventive creators," and PhillyVoice's preview listed Brian Sanders' JUNK among the artists announced ahead of time. I do not have a sourced name for any performer in my photos, so I am leaving names out rather than guess. What I can tell you is what the photos show: a hoop, a spiral, a guitar and a room full of people.

What I respect about FringeArts is the room it makes. It gives artists space to take a chance on something different, including work that will not fit neatly into anyone's idea of what art looks like. Sometimes that is where the magic happens. This city has an astonishing supply of creative people, and a night like this puts them where the rest of us can see them.

## Restaurants, Artists and the Creative Economy

I have spent a lot of time in the hospitality business and, more recently, on ways to grow Philadelphia's creative economy. The more I get involved, the more convinced I am that our restaurants, artists, musicians, theaters and cultural organizations should be working together every chance we get. FEASTIVAL is a clear picture of what that looks like: culinary talent from across the city, people who care about Philadelphia, a cause, and performers sharing the same floor.

The scale is real. The Greater Philadelphia Cultural Alliance's [Prosperity report](https://philaculture.org/what-we-do/research/reports/arts-culture-economic-prosperity/), released in April 2024 for the five county region, put the combined economic impact of Southeastern Pennsylvania's cultural organizations and their audiences at $3.3 billion, and said arts and culture return $1.4 billion in household income to Southeastern Pennsylvania residents. [Creative Philadelphia](https://www.phila.gov/departments/creative-philadelphia/), the city office that used to be called the Office of Arts, Culture, and the Creative Economy, describes its work as amplifying and empowering the city's creative sector through opportunities, support and collaboration. I wrote in September about [why I treat arts funding as an economic question](https://harryhayman.com/blog/philadelphia-arts-funding-one-cent-per-capita), and a benefit that puts restaurants and artists on one floor is that argument with a dress code.

GEMINI Strategic Consultants is proud to support FringeArts, and I am already looking forward to the next one. Culture is infrastructure. On October 28 it looked like a hall full of tables, a guitarist and an aerialist in a hoop. Keep Philly weird, creative and interesting.
