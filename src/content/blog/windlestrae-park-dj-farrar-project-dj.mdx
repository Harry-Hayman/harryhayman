We went to Montgomery Township for a soccer game on Saturday, and I ended up taking a walk that gave me a whole lot more to think about than I expected. I'm Harry Hayman, and I write about places because places keep handing me stories I was not looking for. This one came from William F. Maule Park at Windlestrae, on Kenas Road in North Wales. Most of the leaves had already fallen, but there was still some beautiful color hanging on. I wandered the trails, stopped by the water, and enjoyed a little late November peace and quiet. Somewhere along the way I learned about a young man named DJ Farrar, and about the quilts his family has been making in his name ever since.

## A Big Park With a Local Name

Windlestrae is not a small neighborhood green. When Patch walked readers through it in 2011, it [counted 172 acres in three sections](https://patch.com/pennsylvania/lansdale/spend-the-day-at-windlestrae): the main section off Kenas Road, the Rose Twig section that was once the home of Camp Rose Twig, and the Zehr section reached from Stump Road. That same piece listed what the main section holds: soccer fields, baseball fields, picnic pavilions, a disc golf course, a walking trail and a pond. The trail and the pond are where I spent my time.

The name on the sign honors a neighbor. According to the same Patch article, the park was renamed for Bill Maule, a longtime township resident who died in 2010, served on the park board and was instrumental in starting many of the township's parks. You can still see the three parts of the place in the way [Montgomery Township lists its parks](https://www.montgomerytwp.org/parks-recreation/parks/) today: William F. Maule Park at Windlestrae, then Windlestrae Rose Twig and the Windlestrae Zehr Tract, each with its own entry.

I like that. A township could have named its biggest park after a developer or a highway. It named it after the man who helped build the park system. That tells you something about the people who live there before you meet any of them.

## Who DJ Farrar Was

DJ was just 13 years old when he passed away in 2003 after a two year battle with brain cancer. The Montgomery Township Baseball & Softball Association, which everyone there calls MTBSA, keeps his story on its [DJ Farrar Tournament page](https://mtbsa.teamsnapsites.com/about-us/dj-farrar-tournament/), and it is worth reading in full. It says he played in the recreational and travel programs from T-ball through AAA, and that his life was cut short on January 15, 2003.

The page reads less like a memorial and more like a coach remembering a kid he loved. It describes a player who would run past an opponent while switching sides and tell him "good play" or "great hit". It describes a boy who would hound his parents to throw with him all year, and a father who once brought a ball and gloves to wrestling practice in January and surprised him with a catch in the gym. DJ's answer, according to MTBSA, was "SWEET!"

Then the hard part. The same page says that after surgery, with double vision and poor balance, he still played first base, stepped into the batter's box and pitched a few innings. When chemotherapy took more out of him, he coached first base and helped his friends any way he could.

## The Tournament That Comes Back to Windlestrae Each Summer

Here is the connection I did not know when I was walking those trails. The baseball tournament in DJ's name is played at the park I was standing in. MTBSA's [tournament rules](https://mtbsa.teamsnapsites.com/dj-farrar-rules/) tell each team manager to check in at the Tournament Director's Table by the snack stands at Windlestrae Park, William F Maule Field. Every player receives a tournament t shirt, and a limited supply is sold during the tournament to coaches, parents and families.

Tournament week also has a skills competition. MTBSA's [skills competition page](https://mtbsa.teamsnapsites.com/skill-competition/) for the 2025 edition asked teams to gather by the Windlestrae Pavilion for an opening ceremony where DJ Farrar's family speaks and the DJ Farrar Memorial Award is presented. It asks every player to wear the tournament shirt with his team hat, and it says why in capital letters: this is something special for the family. It even asks teams that are not staying for the competition to come to the ceremony anyway, because it connects the players with the cause.

So the quiet pond I walked past in November is, for one week each summer, one of the busiest corners of the township, full of 10 and 12 year old ballplayers and their parents, all of them there because of one boy. The tournament honors three things MTBSA says DJ lived by: sportsmanship, effort and attitude.

## Project D.J. and More Than 10,000 Quilts

His story didn't end there. His grandmother started Project D.J., a quilting group that has made and donated more than 10,000 quilts to sick and injured children and adults. Think about that. More than 10,000 acts of kindness, all connected to the memory of one young boy.

The public record describes the same work in plainer words. When the Pennsylvania House took up a resolution in DJ's name, its [text](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/text/HTM/2015/0/HR0934/PN3543) called Project DJ "an organization created by DJ's family that makes blankets and quilts for pediatric cancer patients." The same resolution says that since its start the tournament had raised approximately $25,000 for the oncology ward of the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia and for Project DJ. If you want to see what that money supports, the hospital's [Cancer Center pages](https://www.chop.edu/centers-programs/cancer-center) describe the pediatric cancer team that treats children there.

I keep thinking about the math of a quilt. Somebody picks fabric, cuts it, pieces it, sews it and hands it to a family they will probably never see again. Multiply that by more than 10,000 and you have a whole community quietly showing up for strangers, year after year, all in DJ's name.

## The Week Pennsylvania Named for Him

In 2016, State Representative Todd Stephens, who represented the area in Harrisburg, circulated a [memo to every House member](https://www.palegis.us/house/co-sponsorship/memo?memoID=20357&document=HR934) asking them to cosponsor a resolution honoring DJ ahead of that summer's 14th annual tournament. The result was House Resolution 934. According to the [House record for HR 934](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2015/hr934), it designated the week of July 3 through 9, 2016, as "DJ Farrar Week" in Pennsylvania, and it was adopted on June 21, 2016, by a vote of 197 to 0.

Patch reported at the time that the [resolution was presented to the Farrar family](https://patch.com/pennsylvania/lansdale/dj-farrar-week-declared-pa-honor-late-montgomery-twp-man) before the tournament that week. I don't know many things that get 197 votes and none against in Harrisburg. A 13 year old first baseman from Montgomery Township did.

## Why DJ's Story Hit Home for Harry Hayman

And this one hit close to home for me. DJ's story reminded me of my brother, Gray Bradley Hayman, and why the work we do through the Gray Bradley Hayman Foundation means so much to me. Its public filings, which you can see on [ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/237927519), list me among its trustees. It carries my brother's name for the same reason Project D.J. carries DJ's.

When you lose someone you love, you don't stop loving them. You find ways to carry that love forward. Sometimes that means helping another family, supporting another child, or simply making sure someone else's name and story aren't forgotten.

That is what I saw in Montgomery Township. A park named for the neighbor who built the parks. A tournament on those same fields where DJ's family stands up and tells a crowd of young players who he was. A quilting group that turned grief into more than 10,000 blankets for children in hospital beds. None of it is loud. All of it is deliberate.

I came to Montgomery Township for a soccer game. I left thinking about family, community, and the incredible things people do to honor those they've lost. Funny how a simple walk can do that.

If you find yourself near William F. Maule Park at Windlestrae, take a little time to explore. Walk the trail, stop by the water, and if it happens to be July, stay for a few innings of the DJ Farrar Tournament. It's a beautiful place, and there's a meaningful story behind it.
