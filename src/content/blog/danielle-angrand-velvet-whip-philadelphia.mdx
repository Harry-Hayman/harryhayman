Danielle Angrand has the microphone in one hand, an orange cardigan over a black dress, and red curtains behind her. In my photograph, the keyboard player is partly hidden by a music stand. Pull back and there is a saxophonist, a drummer, and a neon eye glowing above the band. That was my Friday night at the Velvet Whip in Philadelphia. I'm Harry Hayman, and Danielle's voice made me stop what I was doing and listen. The [club's October 30 event listing](https://www.tickettailor.com/events/velvetwhip/2414136) called the show a Brian Morris Jazz Trio featuring Danielle Angrand. My own reaction was less restrained. HOLY SMOKES. There is a particular pleasure in hearing somebody you want to tell your friends about while you are still in the room.

## The Name on the Chalkboard

The chalkboard deserves a little attention. Velvet Whip is written across it in pink, with an arrow pointing up and Brian Morris ft/ Danielle Angrand below. The time is 6:30 to 8:30 pm. I photographed the sign along with the musicians because a night like this ought to leave you with the names, too.

The [Philly Jazz Scene listing for that performance](https://www.phillyjazzscene.com/event-details-registration/brian-morris-featuring-danielle-angrand-happy-hour-organ-night-velvet-whip-arts-social-club) fills in the band: Brian Morris Jr. on saxophone, Danielle Angrand on vocals, Bill Schilling on organ, and Tom Cohen on drums. The listing calls it Happy Hour Organ Night. Those details help me look again at the photographs. The keyboard behind Danielle belongs to a player with a name; so does the drum kit at the other side of the stage.

Brian's own musical path includes time running the late night jam at Chris' Jazz Cafe and playing with Mike Boone, as the [South Jersey Jazz Society's profile](https://southjerseyjazz.org/event/young-lions-jazz-feat-brian-morris-jr/) explains. Reading that, I think about how many evenings of playing together can sit behind the few hours an audience gets to hear. A bill gives us the names at the top. Following those names can lead you into another room, another jam, another musician worth hearing. That is one reason I like keeping the chalkboard in the photographs. It gives the next person somewhere to start.

## A Voice with More Than One Starting Point

What caught my attention in Danielle's singing was the beautiful tone, the musical instincts, and a maturity that made me want to keep listening. I kept thinking about Sarah Vaughan. That is my comparison, and I do not throw it around lightly. Danielle also sounded like someone developing her own voice, which is the part that makes me curious about what she does next.

There is some useful background in [Faena's profile of Danielle Angrand](https://www.faena.com/miami-beach/things-to-do/event-calendar/faena-jazz-series-with-danielle-angrand-featuring-shelly). It identifies her as a Haitian American vocalist, describes her classical voice training, and names the Morgan State University Gospel Choir and Schmidt Vocal Arts among the programs through which she has studied. It also says she is continuing her studies at Temple University. That helps explain the range of musical experience she brings to a stage without pretending that a biography can tell you what hearing her feels like.

YoungArts has been paying attention, too. The official [YoungArts winners directory](https://youngarts.org/winners-directory/) lists Danielle Angrand as a 2026 Winner with Distinction in Voice/Jazz. Good! I am pleased to see the credit attached to her name, and I would have been just as excited about the voice if I had gone home knowing nothing about the award.

There is another date to keep in mind. The [Greater Miami and Miami Beach event calendar](https://www.miamiandbeaches.com/event/faena-jazz-series-danielle-angrand-featuring-shelly-berg/43028) lists Danielle with pianist Shelly Berg at Faena Theater on March 3, 2027. That is a future performance, separate from the Philadelphia night I am writing about. It gives anyone whose interest has been caught another place to follow her work. For now, my photograph is of Danielle here, microphone in hand, and that is plenty to remember.

## A Room with Several Kinds of Art on the Calendar

The Velvet Whip puts jazz, blues, opera, cabaret, comedy, soul, indie, and experimental music together on [its own website](https://velvetwhipphilly.com/). I like seeing a room leave itself that much room. Someone may come for a kind of music they already love and find a reason to try something else the next time.

There is a person behind that choice. In [Drink Philly's look inside the Velvet Whip](https://philly.thedrinknation.com/articles/read/17223-Velvet-Whip-Inside-Look-at-the-New-Mixed-Arts-Speakeasy-Performance-Space), Michelle Cryder identifies founder Echo as an opera singer and fine artist, and describes a program that includes jazz, blues, burlesque, theater, comedy, and open mic nights. The article also describes the vintage couches and chandeliers. You can understand why someone with that combination of interests would want a space where different performers could share a stage.

My photographs stay with one part of that program. Red fabric runs behind the players, with fringe above it. The neon eye sits over the saxophonist. Audience members and the backs of their seats fill the foreground. In one frame Danielle extends her free hand toward the band. In another, Brian is playing with the drummer visible to his right. There is a lot happening inside a fairly tight picture.

I LOVE finding places in Philadelphia that give artists a chance to do their thing. That was the excitement behind my post. At the Velvet Whip, I had a singer I wanted to hear more of and a room I wanted other people to know about. Both names belonged in the same message.

## The Hospitality Habit Behind Harry Hayman Creative

After spending a good part of my life around music, musicians, restaurants, and hospitality, I keep coming back to the same practical question: what would make somebody return? A great performance can do it. So can the feeling that there might be another great performance here by somebody whose name you have not learned yet.

The Velvet Whip gave me that feeling. Danielle caught my attention, and then I found myself thinking about the people who had put the night together. Somebody has to give a performance a date and a place. The musicians have to be there together. The audience has to make a plan and come through the door. I can look at the chalkboard and see those arrangements reduced to a few lines of colored writing.

That is why I get excited about places willing to take chances on what an evening can be. I want artists to have somewhere to perform, experiment, and meet an audience. From the audience side, I want a reason to put the phone away and listen. Those wishes met in this room.

I think about that when I say we need more of these places. For my part, that means making another plan, buying a ticket, and telling a friend the name of the person I heard. It is easy to say Philadelphia has talent. Getting somebody else to put Danielle Angrand in their calendar is a useful thing I can do with that enthusiasm.

## Leave with a Name, Come Back with a Friend

For another visit, I would start with the club's current program and [the Velvet Whip's own visitor questions](https://velvetwhipphilly.com/vwqa). The club places itself near 11th and Wood Streets in Philadelphia and explains its membership and ticket holder arrangements there. That is the practical part of turning enthusiasm into an actual night out: look at what is on, check the details, and make the plan.

Then leave yourself open to the part you cannot plan. I knew I enjoyed finding places that do something a little different. I still got a tremendous kick out of hearing Danielle. Having spent years around musicians does not use up that feeling, thank goodness.

The photograph I keep returning to has her smiling into the microphone, the organ player just behind her, and that orange cardigan against the red curtain. I want to hear where she takes that voice. Danielle Angrand. Remember the name, and take a friend who has not heard her yet.
